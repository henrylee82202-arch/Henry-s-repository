# *Leviathan*—Thomas Hobbes

*Leviathan*, written by Thomas Hobbes in 1651, is a foundational text in political philosophy. In it, he argues that our nature is brute, our nature fixed, and most our lives are expended in bloody conflict for impersonal ends. 
## Why I love this cover

Like most penguin classic covers, this one draws from the original source; the visage of leviathan ruling over a miniaturized village adorns the frontispiece of the original 1651 copy. It has staying power for a few reasons: 
* The image itself is a powerful symbol of the subjugation of the masses.
* The antique quality folds the reader into its time, enticing him or her with its old-world illustration.
### Excerpts

> “Life is nasty, brutish, and short”

> “For such is the nature of man, that howsoever they may acknowledge many others to be more witty, or more eloquent, or more learned; Yet they will hardly believe there be many so wise as themselves: For they see their own wit at hand, and other mens at a distance.”

> “The condition of man . . . is a condition of war of everyone against everyone”
