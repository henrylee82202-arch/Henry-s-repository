# *JR*— William Gaddis

After the 20 year long absence following his near thousand-page behemoth debut "The Recognitions", author William Gaddis publishes his second novel, JR. Chaos ensues. 

Written in almost all dialogue, the book follows a Dickensian cast of characters—all of whom bear closer resemblance to Dostoevsky's madmen—centered around a young boy, JR, and his quest to build his fraudulent empire on top of the wrecked heap of American hyper-capitalism of the 1970s.
## Why I love this cover

Though clear debt to Warhol is probably off-putting to most readers by now, I think the connection here is fairly logical—even inevitable—when considering the book's subject:
* Egregious self-aggrandizement at the expense of everyone around you is boiled down to that most cold, rudimentary symbol of life: the dollar. 
* The money sign itself figures heavily in the book as a representation of all that is thin, papery, distrustful, fungible, and flammable about modern relations, which also makes this entry a good match for the [[Contextual|Contextual]] section.

> “Has it ever occurred to any of you that all this is simply one grand misunderstanding? Since you're not here to learn anything, but to be taught so you can pass these tests, knowledge has to be organized so it can be taught, and it has to be reduced to information so it can be organized do you follow that? In other words this leads you to assume that organization is an inherent property of the knowledge itself, and that disorder and chaos are simply irrelevant forces that threaten it from the outside.
