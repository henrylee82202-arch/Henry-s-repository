# *To Kill a Mockingbird*—Harper Lee

The definitive American novel of racial injustice and tremor-inducing popcorn reading in classrooms for decades, *To Kill a Mockingbird* follows the story of Scout, the young daughter of public defender Atticus Finch, as he tries to set an example defending a convicted black man in the racist south. 
## Why I love this cover

The storybook oak where Jem and Scout find Boo Radley's gifts is a common choice, but a few things set this cover apart: 
* The blood-redness of the negative space representing the violent canvas of the world of the south.
* The crooked ink tree with leaves like scales is a welcome enclave of artistic flair in the gray ocean of drab TKAM covers, especially the anniversary ones.
* Despite the conspicuous danger these elements promise–the red-pervading and hangman's tree—there is an odd, oriental calm to the composition. The brushstroke type only adds to the ambience, as if it were the artist's own signature on her solitary masterpiece.
